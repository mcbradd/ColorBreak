Methodology

ColorBreak estimates Magic: The Gathering break outcomes. It is a decision aid, not a prediction of the next opening or a guarantee of value.

Sources and evidence

MTGJSON supplies versioned product identities, contents, and weighted booster sheets. Scryfall supplies exact-printing metadata, images, and published timestamped price observations. Narrow corrections cite official Wizards product or collecting guides. Evidence for product identity, contents, collation, finish, break rules, and price freshness is tracked separately.

ColorBreak always gives its best available answer and improves it as better data arrives. Current exact observations take precedence over matching cached values, followed by a disclosed approximation. Stale or incomplete data never removes a bid estimate. Tap the circled asterisk beside a value or graphic for a short explanation of what could change it.

Expected value and outcomes

Analytic EV sums each possible printing's price times expected copies. Outcome distributions use deterministic, seeded sampling of mutually exclusive variants, sheet choices, fixed cards, nested products, finishes, and duplicate rules. MIN and MAX are calculated directly from possible pack choices, positive-weight variants, guaranteed cards, quantities and duplicate rules. They include rare outcomes even when sampling never draws them. For a random spot, MIN and MAX span the remaining eligible slots. The quick preview already shows these bounds while its typical value is refined. Missing information or approximate pack rules can change the limits. Sampled results use 10,000 openings and can refine to 50,000. The same break state and price snapshot reproduce the same result.

Where MTGJSON marks a sheet colour balanced, sampling honours the one guarantee the flag carries: the sheet spends its first five picks on one card of each mono colour, then draws the rest by the sheet's own printed weights. Wizards has never published the balancing algorithm, so ColorBreak claims no stronger per-colour distribution than that, and a sheet whose resolved printings have lost a whole colour is reported as an omission rather than balanced by invention. MIN and MAX honour the same guarantee.

The Outcome Fingerprint emphasizes median and downside alongside mean EV. A 20-dot display represents modeled frequency in five-percent increments. “Chance to clear” compares modeled counted card value with the entered landed cost.

Ignore bulk under

The bulk threshold excludes individual card finishes priced below the chosen amount from every counted-value calculation and visualization. It is a value filter, not a claim about liquidity, condition, fees, or the ability to sell a card.

Missing information

Missing information qualifies the answer instead of hiding it. If exact pack grouping is missing, a disclosed model samples cards independently using their available prices and expected copies; real packs may vary differently. Generic pack recipes are estimates, not confirmed product contents. When no defensible value or rare-card chance is available, that part contributes $0 to the known subtotal, with an explanation that it is not worthless. Prices do not silently cross printings or foil classes.

Seller estimates and actual results

Planning uses your acquisition costs first, available market prices next, and a disclosed $0 for unknown costs. Missing costs can understate break-even and overstate profit. Recorded-so-far results use recorded revenue and the best cost assumptions; they become actual profit only when receipts, costs and shipments reconcile. Target asks never become recorded sales.

Compliance

Seller scenarios have a dated platform-policy state. Prohibited Whatnot mechanics cannot generate show notes. Approval-required mechanics cannot export without a recorded approval reference. Policy classifications are informational and should be rechecked before each show.
